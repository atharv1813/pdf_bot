[1] FINAL      -> Relativity refers to the revolutionary theories of physics developed by Albert Einstein in the early 20th century. There are two main theories:

## Special Relativity (1905)
This theory deals with objects moving at constant speeds, particularly at speeds close to the speed of light. Key principles include:
- **The speed of light is constant** in all reference frames, regardless of the motion of the light source or observer
- **Time dilation**: Time passes more slowly for objects moving at high speeds relative to a stationary observer
- **Length contraction**: Objects appear shorter in the direction of motion when moving at high speeds
- **Mass-energy equivalence**: Expressed in the famous equation E=mc², showing that mass and energy are interchangeable

## General Relativity (1915)
This theory extends special relativity to include gravity and acceleration. Key concepts include:
- **Gravity is not a force** but rather a curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy
- Massive objects like stars and planets bend the fabric of spacetime around them
- Objects follow curved paths (geodesics) through this curved spacetime, which we perceive as gravitational attraction
- Predictions include gravitational time dilation, gravitational lensing, and the existence of black holes

Both theories fundamentally changed our understanding of space, time, gravity, and the universe. They've been confirmed by numerous experiments and observations and form the foundation of modern physics.
